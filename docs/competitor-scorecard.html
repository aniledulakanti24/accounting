Where UnifiedTree leads
  • Books that add up: invoices, bills, receipts, credit notes and stock all post double-entry journals. Profit & loss, balance sheet and cash flow come from the ledger.
  • GST from the books: GSTR-1 in the GSTN layout, GSTR-3B with statutory credit set-off, GSTR-2B matching, IRNs calculated with the official formula, and e-way bills.
  • Banking and stock: bank rules, CSV statements, a reconciliation statement, FIFO or average costing, batches with expiry, assembly, warehouses and reorder to purchase order.
  • Reach and control: 133 languages, 197 countries, access per page, maker-checker approvals, two-factor sign-in, encrypted backups, 16 apps and an OpenAPI file.
What still holds it back
  • The backend runs in the browser. Backups and 2FA work, but there's no hosted server yet.
  • Filings, IRNs and e-way bills follow the official rules but aren't sent to the real GSTN, IRP or e-way bill portal.
  • Bank feeds, payment gateways and the 16 apps are simulated connections.
  • No SOC 2 or ISO 27001 certification, and the partner directory is sample data.
  • Translations are machine-made, and there's no mobile app.
Path from 2.5 to 4.5
  1. Host the backend: run the same API on a server with a real database. The app only needs client.js pointed at it.
  2. Certified connections: a GST filing partner, the e-invoice registry and the e-way bill portal, then HMRC, ZATCA and others.
  3. Live money: Account Aggregator, Plaid or TrueLayer for banks, and Razorpay or Stripe for payments.
  4. Trust and reach: security certification, reviewed translations, real partner firms and a mobile app.

Code can't fix these: they need hosting, licences, contracts and customers. Steps 1–3 would lift "usable today" to about 4.
